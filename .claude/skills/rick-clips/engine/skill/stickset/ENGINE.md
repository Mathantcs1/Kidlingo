# Stickset: script in, finished YouTube video out

Flat 2D stick-figure characters acting in lit 3D sets. Everything is drawn, voiced, scored and mixed by code; no video-generation model, no stock footage. Technique source: `analysis/REFERENCE-ANALYSIS.md`. Stories and characters are always original (never copy someone else's story, characters, handle or UI).

## Before the first film
1. **Doctor:** `sh skill/stickset/scripts/doctor.sh --install` (needs Node 20+, ffmpeg 7+, Python 3.11+; installs a static ffmpeg and Lemo's core + voice deps, about 0.9 GB). It prints `LIB=<path>`; export it. If a download host is blocked it says which; do not bypass.
2. **Channel brief:** open `skill/stickset/profiles/<channel>.md`. If it says `STATUS: UNFILLED`, ask the channel owner the questions in it, write THEIR answers in, then set `STATUS: READY`. Never invent a channel's tone, cast or rules. `new-film.sh` refuses an unfilled profile.

## Make a film
1. `sh skill/stickset/scripts/new-film.sh <name> --channel <pride-parables|brandwake> --format <short|long>`
   - `short` = 1080×1920 @ 30 fps, 30–60 s. `long` = 1920×1080 @ 24 fps, 2–8 min, rendered as 20–40 s chapters and joined.
   - **`--style sketch` (the channels' own look):** every character and object is hand-sketched in code (graphite line, offset colour on paper grain), sets are depth planes seen by a 2.5D multiplane camera with parallax and rack focus, captions are a hand-lettered word on a marker tag. Cast: `rosa`, `dario`, `neighbours` (bea, tomas, mina); sets: `bakery`, `street`; faces: flat, smug, shock, joy; no `overlay`. **Scenes follow the voice-over (the most important rule):** every phrase gets the place that shows what it says, not two rooms re-zoomed. Scenes (`set=`): bakery, street, shopfront (the shop, night), bench (rows of identical loaves), desk (recipe cards, wrapped loaves, jar), clock (5 a.m., dawn window), calendar (days crossed off from when it first appears), queue (a line of people receding), promise (wrapped loaf + glowing wax seal), talk (word-of-mouth bubbles), finale (morning, confetti). Cast: rosa, dario, neighbours (bea, tomas, mina), crowd (7 people; a queue in `queue`). `prepare` runs a **storyboard check and stops** (override: `VARIETY=off`) when a ~35 s film uses fewer than 5 scenes (1 per ~7 s), too few cameras, one scene over 40 % of the shots, the same camera on two consecutive shots, or the same scene+camera repeated within 4 shots. Plan the storyboard first: list each phrase and the picture that shows it; add new scenes in `core/sketchsets2.js` + `core/sketchlooks.js` when the story needs one.

**Cinematic pacing (what makes it feel like a film, not slides):** cut a voice line into fast shots with inline beat tags placed right before the word that should start the new shot: `Rosa opened {look=window cam=push} a tiny bakery with {cam=truck} one rule.` Aim for a shot every ~1–1.5 s; each beat starts ON its word (checked) and a shot under 0.5 s is refused (look at the word times in `voice/words.json` to place beats). A beat inherits the previous shot, gets a different camera if you give none, and cuts hard by default (a set change turns the page). `look=` cuts to an insert of a set object: bakery oven, shelf, window, counter, basket, mug; street lamp, moon, houses, tree (applies to that beat only). Cameras (every one really moves): wide, push, pull, face (aims at the lead = first name in `cast=`), high, low, truck (lateral dolly), crane (rises), fly (fast push through the planes), dutch (tilted push), orbit (arc around the subject), vertigo (dolly-zoom), tiltup (ground to sky), spiral (push + roll), whip (fast pan with motion blur), **crash** (punch-in on a beat, then a slow creep), **ecu** (extreme close-up), **drift** (steadicam float), **reveal** (a foreground object slides away while focus pulls onto the subject), **follow** (tracking shot: the camera trails a moving character with operator lag, never overshooting); close-ups rack focus. **Operator behaviour:** moves accelerate and settle with a hair of overshoot, handheld is aperiodic noise (not a sine) with a small jolt on every hard cut, and every shot gets a film finish (per-scene colour grade, soft glow, vignette, grain). **Sharpness rule:** a fast cut must land sharp: shots under ~1 s start in focus (only shots over 1.6 s pull focus from soft) and whips use a thin motion streak, not a blur. Measure with `.venv-bpy/bin/python tests/sharpness.py <film.mp4> <timeline.json>` (sharpness by transition type, 1.00 = the film's median; whips should stay above ~0.8). **Coverage rule:** the storyboard check also requires a mix of shot sizes (wide / medium / close / extreme, see `SHOT_SIZE` in `core/sketchcams.js`) and refuses four of one size in a row: open with an establishing wide, punctuate with extreme close-up inserts, end on a wide. `frame=left|right` puts the subject on a third (that beat only). Set changes inside a voice line use quick transitions (whip, scribble, ink in rotation); the page turn (`flip`) is for a new narration line. The world is alive: handheld drift and roll, twinkling stars, drifting clouds, chimney smoke, fireflies, flour dust, flickering oven glow, neighbours walk in, characters blink and their lead talks on the spoken words. At most one of rosa/dario may share a shot with the neighbours. Works in both formats (sizes follow the frame's short side). **Each style accepts only the transitions its page can draw (others are rejected with the valid list). Transitions default to the sketch language:** set change → `flip` (page turn), move into a close-up → `ink` (ink bleed from the character), other same-set move → `scribble` (pencil scribble wipe); override with `cut=cut|fade|whip|flip|ink|scribble` (classic: `cut|fade|cctv|whip|punch`). `--style classic` (default) keeps the earlier look. Design sheet: `films/design-sheet`.
2. **Write `films/<name>/script.md`.** Each block is one shot: a directive line, then the narration for that shot:
   `[set=bakery cam=face face=smug cast=baker overlay=cctv cut=fade]`
   - **Classic style:** `set` (cellblock, bakery, street; carries over), `cam` (wide, push, face, high, low), `face` (flat, smug, shock), `cast` (baker, neighbours, none), `overlay=cctv`. **Sketch style has its own vocabulary (see `--style sketch` above):** rosa/dario/neighbours, faces incl. joy, no overlay. Any other directive key or value is rejected with the valid list (typos never fall back silently).
   - **Characters:** only `baker` and `neighbours` exist. A film that needs its own cast must add the character to `figures()` in its `index.html`, its name to `core/vocab.js` (`CAST_NAMES`) and, if it needs a look, to `core/rig.js`. Ask the channel owner for the cast; do not invent it.
   - **Transitions come from the script, and every boundary gets one** (a bare hard cut only when you write `cut=cut`). Each is centred on the first spoken word of the new shot: set change → `fade` (dissolve); a same-set move into a close-up (`cam=face`) → `punch` (blurred zoom + flash); any other same-set move → `whip` (slide wipe with motion blur); `overlay=cctv` → `cctv` (glitch cut into the camera view; its frame fades out when the next shot starts). Override any boundary with `cut=cut|fade|cctv|whip|punch`. Vary them: avoid the same transition three times in a row.
   - One idea per block, hook in the first 3 seconds, one turn, an ending that echoes. Write a `TREATMENT.md` (three structures, logline, shot list, cue map, sound table) before drawing.
3. **Draft, then final:** `LIB=<lib> sh films/<name>/build.sh draft` (half size), look at stills and frame strips, fix, then `sh films/<name>/build.sh`. The build runs voice (Kokoro, offline), a speech-to-text check of every line, the cue-map check, render, mix, mux at −14 LUFS, and writes `subtitles_en.srt`.
4. **Render budget:** after the voice step and before rendering, the build prints an estimate (calibrated: ~0.08 s per 1080×1920 frame on this container, no GPU). If it exceeds **2 hours** the build **stops (exit 3)**: tell the user, offer a shorter film or a lower-resolution cut, and only rerun with `CONFIRM_LONG_RENDER=1` if they agree. Treat it as an estimate. Long builds can outlast a tool call's time limit: run `build.sh` in the background and poll its log.
5. **Check before delivering:** run `node --test tests/*.test.mjs` with `LEMO_OPUSCAR_HOME` set (includes a render smoke test that fails on blank-paper frames); then `sh skill/stickset/scripts/check-video.sh <mp4> <w> <h> <fps> <seconds>` (size, fps, duration, no black frames); view contact sheets and 0.2 s strips of key cuts; confirm every caption is readable and not covering the face.
6. **Deliver:** `film-*.mp4`, `poster.jpg`, `subtitles_en.srt`, `TREATMENT.md`, `CREDITS`, and filled `metadata.md` (title, description, tags, thumbnail concept) in the channel's conventions. Tell the user where the files are and that they are responsible for the materials they use.

## Captions
One word at a time, from the script's own words timed by the voice check (a word appears exactly when it is spoken; a caption never crosses a shot cut; punctuation-only tokens are dropped; numbers heard as several words span them, and the build warns when timing had to be spread), centred at 64.5 % of the height, heavy white with black outline, minimum hold 0.25 s.

## Known limits (state them, do not hide them)
Sets are stylised, not photoreal. Look on a real phone, real GPU rendering and uploading to YouTube are untested. Silences and J/L-cuts in the mix are designed but not measured. Adding a set means a new `core/sets/<name>.js` plus a name in `core/sets/names.js` and `core/sets/index.js`.
