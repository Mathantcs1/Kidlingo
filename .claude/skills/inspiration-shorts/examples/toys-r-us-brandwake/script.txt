Toys R Us sold more than eleven billion dollars of toys in one year. And it still went bankrupt. Here is the bill. Four hundred fifty-seven million dollars of interest in one year. That is one point two five million dollars a day. In two thousand five, KKR, Bain Capital and Vornado bought it for six point six billion dollars, plus its debt. By January twenty seventeen, the company owed four point eight billion dollars. Its own measure of operating earnings, adjusted EBITDA, was seven hundred ninety-two million. Interest took fifty-eight percent of it. Sales were slipping too. Down two point two percent. In September twenty seventeen, it filed for Chapter eleven. Six months later, it asked to sell or close all seven hundred thirty-five U.S. stores. Debt was not the only reason. Online rivals and a weak holiday hurt too. That is my read, not a court finding.
