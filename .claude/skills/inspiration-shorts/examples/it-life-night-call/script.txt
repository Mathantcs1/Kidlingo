I was one step out of the door for dinner when my phone started to buzz.
A production issue. Eleven people on the bridge call. Everyone talking, nobody sure who owned it. I sat on the floor in my jacket and opened my laptop. My dinner reservation became a very long dinner at my own desk.
After two hours, I found it. One line. One wrong value in a configuration. I changed it, we tested it, and the bridge went quiet. Someone said, "Thanks, everyone." And eleven people dropped off one by one.
Nobody sent a message. Nobody remembered the one line. The only message I received was from home. "We ordered for you."
In two thousand fifteen, you fixed it and told nobody. In two thousand twenty-six there is a chat thread, a ticket, a postmortem, and an AI summary of what happened. We document more now. But the feeling of being the one who fixed it at night has not changed.
And the best part? Someone kept the food warm. Tell me, what was your longest night call?
