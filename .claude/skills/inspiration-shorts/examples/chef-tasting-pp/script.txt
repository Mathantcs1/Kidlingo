He had cooked the same soup ten thousand times. So one day, Ace stopped tasting it.
Pride loved this. "You're the chef. The soup knows what to do."
His restaurant held four point eight stars. Pride kept a very smug counter.
Week one. A new salt supplier. Same label. Sam, the line cook, tasted the soup. "Chef, it's off."
"It's not," said Ace. He didn't taste it.
Four point five. "Customers have bad days," whispered Pride.
Four point one. The reviews said too salty. Ace blamed the critics.
Three point six. Sam tried again. "Chef. Just one spoon."
"Do not," hissed Pride.
Then a woman at table four sent her bowl back with a note: "I used to love this place."
Ace read it twice. Then he picked up a spoon, for the first time in forty days.
Salt. So much salt.
He poured out the pot. He wrote on the chalkboard: "We served you bad soup. Sorry. Fixed today."
Three point one. Three point five. Three point nine. The stars came back slowly, one honest spoon at a time.
Pride says: I've cooked this ten thousand times. Humility says: taste it once more.
What are you no longer checking? Every fall begins with one proud decision.
