Nokia's phone software ran on half the world's smartphones. Then it didn't.
That software was called Symbian. In 2008, it was on fifty-two percent of smartphones sold.
Nokia had been the biggest phone maker on Earth since 1998. So why worry?
Because in 2007, Apple launched the iPhone. A year later, the first Android phone arrived.
Symbian slid. Forty-seven percent in 2009. Thirty-eight in 2010.
Nokia's answer, in February 2011? A bet on Microsoft's Windows Phone.
In the last three months of 2011, Symbian was down to about twelve percent.
In 2013, Microsoft agreed to buy Nokia's phone business for about five point four billion euros.
So is Nokia dead? No. Today it sells the equipment that connects AI data centers, and Nvidia invested one billion dollars.
Lesson: a big number is not safety. Check whether it's rising or falling.
