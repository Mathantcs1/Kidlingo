In two thousand fifteen, our testing plan was an Excel sheet with two hundred rows.
Green meant pass. Red meant fail. Yellow meant, "I am not sure." A tester sat for two days, clicking every screen, one by one. And I stood behind him with a coffee, hoping the yellow cells would turn green. When something broke, he turned the screen and said, "Look. I clicked here, and it broke."
Now it is two thousand twenty-six. The pipeline runs two hundred checks in a few minutes. No sheet. No waiting. That is a big improvement.
But when one check fails, nobody is sitting beside me. The pipeline just says, "Failed." No face. No explanation. Just a red box. And I spend an hour finding out whether the bug is in my code, in the test, or in the environment.
And there is another thing. Sometimes everything passes, and we feel safe. But a passing test only proves we tested what we thought of. Not what we forgot.
So tell me, which one did you prefer? The sheet with a person, or the pipeline with a red box?
