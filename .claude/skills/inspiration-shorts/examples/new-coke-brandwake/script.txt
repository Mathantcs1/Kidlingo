Coca-Cola changed its formula because the numbers said to. Seventy-nine days later, it brought the old one back.
The myth is that executives were careless. The record is stranger.
By Coca-Cola's own account, its share lead had been slipping for fifteen years in a row.
So it tested a sweeter formula. The company says nearly two hundred thousand consumers preferred it. One history puts the tests at one hundred ninety thousand. Either way, the new taste won.
April twenty-third, nineteen eighty-five. The new Coke launched. The company's president said he had never been as confident about a decision.
Then the hotline lit up. Sources disagree on how many calls a day. They agree it was far above normal.
Here's what the tests missed. According to History dot com, testers never asked how people would feel if the old formula were replaced.
July eleventh. The original came back as Coca-Cola Classic. Seventy-nine days after launch.
Within a few months, History reports, Classic was the top-selling sugar cola again, ahead of Pepsi.
The data was right about taste. It never measured attachment.
