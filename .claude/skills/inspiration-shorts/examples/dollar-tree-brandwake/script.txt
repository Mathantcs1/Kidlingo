For more than thirty-five years, a Dollar Tree meant one dollar. Then the company said that was over.
Three founders opened it in nineteen eighty-six as Only One Dollar. Five stores, in Georgia, Tennessee and Virginia.
In September twenty twenty-one, Dollar Tree announced a dollar twenty-five price. The company called it permanent, and not a reaction to short-term conditions.
Its stated reason: to return to its historical gross margin as freight and wage costs climbed.
By late February twenty twenty-two, every U.S. store had switched.
It didn't stop there. In March twenty twenty-four, the company said more than three hundred items would run from a dollar fifty up to seven dollars. Most stayed at a dollar twenty-five.
By January, about fifty-three hundred stores had the multi-price format. Retail Dive reports eighty-five percent of items are two dollars or less.
Compare Costco's hot dog. Costco earns about half its operating profit from membership fees, so the hot dog can sit still. Dollar Tree's dollar was the whole model. That's my read, not a company claim.
In October, it opened its nine thousand five hundredth store. Forty years after the first.
