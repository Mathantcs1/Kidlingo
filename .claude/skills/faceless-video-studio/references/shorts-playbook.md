Source: screenshots of channel Shorts grids (titles, thumbnails, view counts only; no video files yet). Goal: learn the process and ADAPT it, never copy titles, characters or IP.

## Patterns to adopt
1. Hook in title AND first second: curiosity gap, a number, a person ("The Man Who...", "He X. 3 Years Later...").
2. Big centre word-by-word captions with one highlighted word (our current captions are small, bottom).
3. Split-screen A vs B with counters (Day 1 / Day 90) is a repeatable, shareable template.
4. Pattern interrupt every 1.5-2.5s (zoom, cut, sfx), loop-friendly ending.
5. Series naming + consistent characters + banner tagline.
6. Heavy-tailed results: publish many variants of proven templates; double down on the winners.

## Risks
- Same template many times can look repetitive (YouTube): vary topics, characters, visuals.
- Kids/"made for kids" setting must be truthful; no third-party IP (Roblox/Undertale characters).
- Screenshots show views, not watch time; do not assume monetization.

## Process (recipe)
1. One-sentence premise with a contrast or a forbidden thing (same start / different choice).
2. 8-14 beats, one line each; each line = one on-screen text change (or one narration chunk).
3. Numbers and day counters change visibly; directions must match meaning.
4. Dark/cold vs warm light signals bad vs good outcome.
5. Hook line in the first second; payoff line last; ending loops to the first frame.
6. Text-only Shorts need no ElevenLabs credit (music + sfx only); narrated ones cost about 400-500 characters.
7. Voice-over stories: 2-4 word caption chunks, one emphasis colour, camera or angle change about every second.
Note: ref audio was recorded from a speaker, so music/sfx quality could not be judged.

Detail from ref 1 (re-check): the "no narration" stick-figure Shorts still carry short voice clips as sound design (phone-call snippets: "Bye.", "That was awesome.", "Thank you."), so audio can tell the story without a narrator. Use short character voice/sfx clips (ElevenLabs sound effects cost credit; prefer free local sfx first).

## Update 21 Nov 2026 (2): four narrated clips (480x360 recordings, 6-12s)
Topics: headless horseman trick-or-treat; insurance scam ("whoever hits you from behind gets the blame"); Japanese pachinko halls; Japanese "banishment rooms" for fired workers.
- Hook = one odd real-world fact in the first sentence ("Every Halloween...", "In Japan...").
- Delivery very fast: about 190-220 wpm (ours about 140-160).
- Captions: ONE word at a time, bottom-centre, white with dark outline, changing with every word.
- New shot or camera move about every 1-1.5s: 2.5D fly-through / dolly zoom (neon street, hallway tunnel of slot machines), saturated single-colour scenes (neon night, red casino, blue office).
- Simple cartoon characters with iconic props that carry a text label (red FIRE button).
- Sentences short; payoff reveal late in the clip ("Just a ticking clock and a guy with his feet up.").
Two families so far: A = text-only split screen/counters/loop (no narration), B = fast narrated cartoon story with one-word captions and camera flights.
Adaptation for us: raise narration pace (ElevenLabs speed up to 1.1-1.2 if quality holds), one-word captions, shot every 1-1.5s, camera dolly per shot, saturated scene colour per beat; verify every real-world claim before use (sources in description).
