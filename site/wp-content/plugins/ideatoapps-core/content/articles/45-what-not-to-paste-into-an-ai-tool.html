Most of what people paste into AI apps is fine: a recipe, a draft email, a paragraph from their own essay. A few details should never go in, and some need trimming first. Before you paste, decide which of three groups it belongs to.

Fine to paste as it is

Your own writing with no private details in it is fine. So are general questions, public information and examples you made up. "Help me make this birthday invitation sound friendlier" needs no trimming at all.

[ita_why title="Where your text goes after you send it"]

Unless the model runs on your own device, what you type goes to the provider's servers. It's processed there and usually stored for a time. How long depends on the provider and your settings, and a deleted chat may take a while to disappear from their systems.

In some services, people review a sample of chats, for example to check safety. Some may use chats to train later models unless you turn that off. If the app is connected to other services, like email, parts of your request may go there too. The app's privacy page says what applies to you.

[/ita_why]

Remove details first

Take more care with health and money details, anything about your children, and messages other people wrote. You can get the same help without the personal parts. Use roles instead of names, describe a document instead of pasting it, and crop screenshots to the part you need.

A parent filling in a school form may find one question about their child's allergy hard to answer. Pasting the whole form would share the child's name, school and date of birth. Typing this instead gets the same help: "My nine-year-old has a mild nut allergy and carries medicine on school trips. Help me write two clear sentences for a school form."

At work, "Customer A" works as well as a real name, and the order number can stay out. Paste work documents only if your workplace allows it. Photos and screenshots can show more than you meant, such as a name in a notification or an address on a letter. Some photo files also store where the photo was taken.

Never paste these

Passwords, PINs, one-time login codes, security answers and recovery codes can open your accounts. So can access keys, the long secret strings a program uses to sign in to an account. No app needs any of them to help you.

Full ID and account numbers belong in the same group: passport, driver's license and national ID numbers, and bank card or account numbers. Add anything your workplace or school forbids, for example confidential documents, customer lists or exam papers.

If you paste one of these by mistake, change the password, or cancel and replace the key or recovery codes, at once. Then delete the chat, any shared link to it and anything memory saved. At work, also tell whoever handles IT or security. For a card or ID number, contact the bank or the office that issued it and ask what to do next.

Check your app's settings

Every app names its settings differently, but most have a privacy or data section. Look for these:

  • Whether your chats may be used to train or improve future models, and how to turn that off.
  • How long chats are kept, and how to delete them.
  • Whether memory is on. Memory adds saved details to later chats, and you can usually see and delete what it saved.
  • Whether there's a temporary chat mode that isn't saved to your history, though the provider may still keep it for a short time.
  • Which chats you've shared as links, since anyone with a link can usually open that chat.

At work, use the account your employer provides, if there is one. Work accounts often come with different terms from personal ones.

Try it

Do this with someone who uses an AI app: a parent, a partner or a coworker.

  1. Open the app's settings together and find the privacy or data section.
  2. Note what the settings for training, chat history and memory are set to.
  3. If the app has a memory list, each of you reads your own list privately and deletes anything that shouldn't be there.
  4. Pick one setting either of you wants changed on a personal account, and change it now.
