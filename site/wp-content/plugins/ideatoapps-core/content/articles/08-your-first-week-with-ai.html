A good way to learn an AI chatbot is to use it for small, real tasks, a few minutes a day, for one week. You don't need a course or special skills. By day seven you'll know what it helps you with, where it goes wrong, and which habits keep you safe.

If a day doesn't suit you, swap it, but keep the writing days before the fact-checking days.

Before day one: set up and set rules

Pick one chatbot app and use it for the whole week, so you learn the habits and not a new screen each day. Spend two minutes in its settings. Look for whether chats are saved, whether they may be used to improve the service, and whether there's a memory feature. Change them now if you prefer, or just note where they are.

Then set a few ground rules for yourself. Check them off as you read:

[ita_checklist id="first-week-ground-rules"] [ita_item]Never paste passwords, bank details or ID numbers into any chat.[/ita_item] [ita_item]Remove names and case numbers before pasting a document, and keep private details about customers or family members out.[/ita_item] [ita_item]Facts, numbers and quotes get checked somewhere else before I use them.[/ita_item] [ita_item]Health, legal and money questions go to a qualified person in the end.[/ita_item] [ita_item]Anything I send or publish, I read in full first.[/ita_item] [/ita_checklist]

Days 1 to 3: small writing jobs

Start with everyday writing, where chatbots are often useful and mistakes are cheap.

Day 1, a short message. Ask for help with something you'd write anyway, like a shift-swap request or a reminder for a sports team. Type a [ita_term define="The message or instructions you type to an AI app to tell it what you want."]prompt[/ita_term] that says who it's for, what it must include and how long it should be.

Day 2, fix a draft. Ask for a first draft, then improve it with short follow-ups such as "shorter" or "add the date". A follow-up in the same chat usually beats starting over (see "AI myths that waste your time").

Day 3, tidy your own notes. Paste a messy list, like notes from a parents' meeting or jobs for a work van. Take names and private details out first. Ask for it grouped and cleaned up. The facts are yours, so it's easier to spot anything that was changed or added.

[ita_prompt title="Day 1 starter"] Write a short, friendly message to the parents on my child's soccer team. Must include: Saturday's game starts at [time] at [field name], and players should arrive 20 minutes early. Length: under 50 words. No emojis. [/ita_prompt]

Days 4 to 7: explain, plan, then judge

Day 4, learn something. Ask it to explain a topic you're curious about, in simple words, then ask two follow-up questions. Check one fact from the answer in a reliable source, such as a textbook or an official website.

Day 5, plan something. Ask for a plan: a weekly meal list, steps for clearing out a garage, or an outline for a short report. Plans are a good fit, because you can judge them with common sense.

Day 6, try to catch a mistake. Ask about something you know very well, like your trade, your hometown's history or a hobby. Look for small errors stated with confidence. Spotting one yourself teaches you what to watch for.

Day 7, look back. Scroll through the week. Which tasks saved you time? Which needed so much fixing that you'd rather do them yourself? Write both lists down and keep them for next week.

[ita_why title="Why start with writing and planning, and leave facts for later?"]

Chatbots write by predicting likely text. So they are usually more dependable at the shape of things: a clear message, a tidy list, a sensible plan. You can judge those results yourself in seconds.

Facts are different. A wrong date or name looks just as smooth as a right one. Starting with tasks you can check easily lets you build trust step by step. Day 6 then shows you what a confident mistake looks like before it matters.

  2. Copy the "Day 1 starter" prompt. Fill in the parts in square brackets and change the details to match a real message you need to send.
