When an answer is wrong, it's natural to type "no, try again". That gives the chatbot almost nothing to work with. It knows you're unhappy, but not why, so the next answer is a new guess.

A better reply names the problem, points to where it is, and says what to keep.

Name the problem first

Before you reply, decide what kind of problem it is. Most bad answers are one of five kinds, and each has its own fix.

  • Too generic. It could be for anyone. Add the detail that makes it yours: who it's for, your deadline, what you've tried.
  • Wrong shape or length. Say the shape you want: "five bullet points", "one paragraph", "a table with two columns".
  • Misunderstood the task. Say what it did and what you wanted, in different words from your first [ita_term define="The message or instructions you type to an AI app to tell it what you want."]prompt[/ita_term]. Repeating the same words often gets the same mistake.
  • Wrong tone. Show it. Paste one line that sounds right, or quote the line that sounds wrong.
  • Wrong fact. Don't argue about it in the chat. Check it in your textbook, your notes or another trusted source, then give the chatbot the correct fact.

The earlier article on AI myths explains why a quick fix in the same chat usually beats starting from nothing. This one is about what to write in that fix.

Keep, change, add

Say you asked for study notes on chapter 4 of your history book. Two of the three points are good. The second one is about chapter 3. A three-line reply fixes it:

[ita_prompt title="Keep and change"] Keep: [what worked, for example: the first and third points, and the short sentences] Change: [what's wrong, for example: the second point is about chapter 3 instead of chapter 4] Add: [anything missing, for example: one example from the reading] Rewrite with only these changes. [/ita_prompt]

The "Keep" line matters more than it looks. If you only list problems, the chatbot may rewrite everything, and the good parts can get worse. Telling it what to keep protects them.

You don't need all three lines every time. Often "Keep" and "Change" are enough. Skip "Add" unless something is really missing.

[ita_why title="Why does 'try again' often bring back the same mistake?"]

In one chat, the chatbot can usually see everything so far, including its own bad answer. That answer is now part of what it reads, so the next answer can borrow from it, mistakes included.

"Try again" doesn't add anything new to read. Your original request is still unclear in the same way. A short note about what to keep and what to change gives the chatbot new information to work from.

[/ita_why]

When to stop fixing and start fresh

Sometimes a chat keeps repeating itself. You correct something, and two answers later it's back. Signs it's time for a new chat:

  • The same mistake returns after two clear corrections.
  • The answers keep getting longer and further from what you asked.
  • You changed your mind about the task halfway through.

Start the new chat with a short summary: what you want, the details that matter, and the mistake to avoid. Your Keep and Change lines are a good base for it.

Some apps also let you edit an earlier message and send it again. That replaces the answer that came after it, so the bad answer is no longer in the chat. It's useful when you notice the problem came from your own first message.

Try it

This one takes about two minutes.

  1. Open a chat where an answer wasn't quite right. If you don't have one, ask for a summary of one page of your notes.
  2. Before replying, write one word that names the problem: generic, shape, misunderstood, tone or fact.
  3. Complete the Keep and Change lines. Add the third line only if something is missing.
  4. Send it, and read only the parts you asked to change.
  5. If the same problem returns, open a new chat and paste your Keep and Change lines as the first message.
