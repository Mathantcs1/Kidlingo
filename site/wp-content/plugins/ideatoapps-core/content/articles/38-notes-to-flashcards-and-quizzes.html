With the right [ita_term define="The message or instructions you type to an AI app to tell it what you want."]prompt[/ita_term], a chatbot can turn a page of your notes into flashcards or a short quiz in a few seconds. Tell it to use only your notes, so the cards match what your class covered. Then check every card before you study from it. If you've ever made flashcards by hand the night before a test, you know how much time this can save.

Before you paste

Notes often hold more than facts. Before you paste them into a chatbot, take out:

  • Names of classmates, students or teachers.
  • Grades, comments on a student's work, or anything about a student's health or family.
  • Your own contact details and passwords.

Teachers should also check their school's rules on which apps may be used with class materials. Some schools allow only certain apps, or none, for anything linked to students.

Flashcards from your own notes

This works for lecture notes, textbook summaries and vocabulary lists. Change the numbers in brackets to suit your notes (example values: replace them):

[ita_prompt title="Cards from my notes only"] Make flashcards from my notes below. Use only facts that are in the notes. Write [15] cards. Each card tests one fact. Write each card on one line, in this form: FRONT | BACK Put a question on the front. Keep the back under [12] words. Mix the card types: some definitions, some "why" questions, some examples. If my notes don't have enough for [15] good cards, make fewer. Notes: [paste your notes] [/ita_prompt]

Many flashcard apps can import one card per line, with a separator between front and back. Your app's import help says which separator it expects.

You've probably studied from a card that turned out to be wrong at least once. Cross out any card that fails one of these checks:

  • Is the back in your notes? Even when told to use only your notes, a chatbot can add facts from elsewhere.
  • Does it test one thing? A card with two facts on the back is two cards.
  • Do you have to remember the answer? "Mitosis makes two identical cells: true or false?" can be guessed from the wording. "What does mitosis produce?" can't.

A quiz for your class

Teachers and tutors can use the same idea for a quick class quiz. Use a handout you wrote, or one you're allowed to share. Set the grade and the numbers for your class (example values: replace them):

[ita_prompt title="Quiz from my handout only"] Write a quiz for [grade 7] students from the handout below. Use only facts in the handout. Write [8] questions: [5] multiple choice and [3] short answer. For multiple choice, give four options. Make each wrong option a mistake students often make on this topic. Put all the answers in a key at the end, after the last question. Handout: [paste the handout] [/ita_prompt]

Before you print it, answer every question yourself without the key. Check the wording, the reading level and that each question has only one right answer. A chatbot's quiz can include a question with two defensible answers, or a "wrong" option that is actually right.

[ita_why title="What makes a flashcard work"]

Flashcards help most when you try to recall the answer before you turn the card. Testing yourself this way, and spreading your reviews over several days, are well-studied ways to remember material for longer than rereading.

So keep the front short and the back shorter. Study a card until you get it right once, then review it again after a day or two, and again after a longer gap.

Try it

  1. Choose one page of your notes from this week, and remove names and anything personal.
  2. Paste it into the flashcard prompt above, and ask for ten cards.
  3. Cross out any card whose back isn't in your notes, and any card you can answer from the front's wording alone.
  4. Test yourself on the rest, and mark the ones you missed.
  5. Set a reminder for two days from now to review the marked cards again.
