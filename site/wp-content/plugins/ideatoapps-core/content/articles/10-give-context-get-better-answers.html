Imagine you run a small bakery, and next month you'll close on Mondays. You ask a chatbot for a sign to tell customers. The result is fine, but it could be for any shop anywhere. Nothing in it sounds like you.

The fix is context: the details only you know. Here's the same request, before and after, and a simple way to find what's missing.

Before: the request with no background

Here's the kind of [ita_term define="The message or instructions you type to an AI app to tell it what you want."]prompt[/ita_term] most people type first:

[ita_prompt title="Before"] Write a notice saying we are closed on Mondays. [/ita_prompt]

The chatbot has to guess almost everything. It doesn't know it's a bakery, when the change starts, why it's happening, or who reads the sign. So it picks the most typical choice at every gap. You'd likely get something polite and stiff, maybe with "We apologize for any inconvenience", and nothing about your regulars or your bread.

The chatbot isn't being lazy. It only knows what's in the conversation, plus anything the app adds, like saved memory or a file you shared. It can't see your shop.

After: the same request with context

Now add the details a new employee would need before writing that sign for you:

[ita_prompt title="After"] Write a short notice for the front door of my small neighborhood bakery. From the first of next month, we will be closed on Mondays so the team gets a full weekend-style break. Tuesday to Sunday hours stay the same. Most customers are regulars who come in for bread on their way to work, and some of them come on Mondays. The tone should be warm and a little playful, like the way we talk to people at the counter. Mention that Saturday is a good day to stock up, and thank people for understanding. Keep it under 60 words so it fits on one sheet in large print. [/ita_prompt]

Every line removes a guess. The chatbot now knows the audience (regulars), the reason (rest for the team), the start date, the tone and the size. The draft is much more likely to sound like your bakery and to answer the questions customers will ask.

Useful context usually falls into a few kinds:

  • Who it's for and what they already know.
  • The situation: what happened, what's changing, any deadlines.
  • What you've tried, so you don't get the same idea back.
  • Your style, ideally shown with a short example of your own writing.
  • Limits, like length, where it will be used, or words to avoid.

You don't need all of them each time. Add the ones that would change the answer.

Let the chatbot ask you first

Sometimes you don't know which details matter. A handy trick is to ask the chatbot to interview you before it writes anything.

[ita_prompt title="Ask me first"] I need help writing a notice for my bakery about a change in opening days. Before you write anything, ask me up to five short questions about the details you need. Wait for my answers, then write the notice. [/ita_prompt]

Its questions show you what it would otherwise have guessed. Answer them briefly, and you've added the right context without having to think of it all up front.

[ita_why title="Why can a long chat lose the context you gave early on?"]

A model can only take a limited amount of text into account at once. This limit is called the [ita_term define="The amount of text, counted in small pieces called tokens, that a model can take into account at one time."]context window[/ita_term].

[ita_figure name="context-window" alt="Two bars: early in a chat the space is mostly empty; late in a long chat it is full and the oldest text is cut off." caption="In a very long chat, the oldest text can fall out first."]

In a very long chat, the earliest messages can be dropped, or the model can simply give them less weight. If the chatbot starts ignoring details you gave at the start, repeat the key ones in a new message, or start a fresh chat with a short summary of what matters.

Try it

Use the "Ask me first" idea on one real task in about five minutes.

  1. Pick something you need to write this week, like a note to a client, a message to a school or a short post for a local group.
  2. Copy the "Ask me first" prompt and change the first line to describe your task.
  3. Answer the chatbot's questions in a few words each.
  4. Read the draft and underline one detail that came from your answers. Would it have guessed that on its own?

Save the questions it asked. Next time you write something similar, you can add those details in your first message.
