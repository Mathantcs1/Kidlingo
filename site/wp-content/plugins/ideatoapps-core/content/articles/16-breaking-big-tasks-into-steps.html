Say you're moving your family to a new apartment next month. You ask a chatbot to "plan the whole move". The answer is long. It mentions packing, a moving van, school forms and utilities, and gives each one a single line.

Step 1: Ask for the list, not the work

Start by asking only for the plan. Your first [ita_term define="The message or instructions you type to an AI app to tell it what you want."]prompt[/ita_term] can be this short:

[ita_prompt title="Steps only"] Help me prepare for [the big task, for example: moving my family to a new apartment next month]. List the main steps from now until it's done, in order. Number them, and give each one a short name. Don't do any of the steps yet. [/ita_prompt]

You know things the chatbot doesn't, so change the list to fit your situation:

  • Delete steps you've already done.
  • Join two steps if they're really one job.
  • Add steps it missed. Maybe your child's new school needs a form by Friday.
  • Change the order if your week needs it.

Make these changes before you ask for any of the work.

Step 2: Work on one step at a time

Pick one step, such as packing. Give the chatbot the details for that step only. Say how many rooms you have, who can help, and which evenings are free. Ask for a short answer you can check.

If a date or a number looks wrong in a short answer, you'll see it now, while it's small. In one huge answer, a wrong moving date could change the packing plan, the school form and the day the internet is connected.

A university student can split an essay the same way: choose the question, find sources, write an outline, draft each section, check the references. Send each step as its own message.

Step 3: Repeat what you decided

As you finish steps, keep a short list of what you've decided. Paste it at the top of each new step, so the chatbot works from your choices and doesn't invent new ones.

[ita_prompt title="Next step, with what's decided"] Decided so far: [3 to 5 short lines, for example: moving day, who packs the kitchen, the new school's start date] Now help with step [number]: [step name]. Give me a short draft I can check, under 150 words. [/ita_prompt]

A long chat can lose track of early details; see "Give context, get better answers" for the reason. With this list, you can also start a fresh chat for any step without losing the decisions on your list.

[ita_why title="Why does one huge request go wrong?"]

Every answer has a length limit. When you ask for everything at once, the chatbot has to fit every part into one answer, so each part gets a few lines. Later parts can be rushed, or the answer can stop before the end.

There's also no chance to steer. In one big answer, the chatbot makes every choice itself, one after another. If an early choice is wrong, the later parts build on it. Going step by step lets you make each choice yourself.

[/ita_why]

Not every task needs this. A two-line text to a friend is fine in one message. Split a task when its parts depend on each other. Split it, too, when one long answer would be hard to check.

Try it

Do the first part on paper, before you open any app.

  1. Choose one big task from your month: a university assignment, a job application, a move, or a family event.
  2. Write the steps you can think of, one per line.
  3. Send the "Steps only" prompt. Add any new steps it lists to your paper, in a different color.
  4. Circle the one step you'll do first.
  5. Send the second prompt for that step only, with your "Decided so far" lines filled in.
