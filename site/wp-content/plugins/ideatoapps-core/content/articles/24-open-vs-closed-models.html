An open model is one whose trained files you can download and run on computers you choose. A closed model is one you can use only through its maker's app or service. The choice affects where your data goes, how much setup you take on, and how much control you have over changes.

What "open" usually means

Most models called open are [ita_term define="A model whose trained parameters are published, so others can download and run it."]open-weight[/ita_term] models. The maker publishes the parameters, the numbers the model learned in training. You can run the model yourself, and often adapt it with more training.

The maker may still keep the training data and much of the training code private. And every download comes with a [ita_term define="The terms that say how you are allowed to use something, such as software or a model."]license[/ita_term]. Some licenses allow commercial use. Others limit it, or add conditions such as rules about allowed uses or a requirement to credit the maker.

As "A plain-English AI glossary" puts it, open-weight doesn't always mean free to use any way you like. Read the license of the exact version you plan to use.

For a worker, the closed option often looks simpler: you sign in and use it. For a builder, an open model can mean predictable behavior and data that stays inside your own systems. It also means you run, secure and update it yourself.

There is a mixed option too. Some hosting services run open models for you. Setup is lighter, but your data goes to that host, so read its terms the same way you would read a closed model's.

[ita_why title="Open weights, open code, open data"]

"Open" can describe three different things: the trained parameters, the code used to train the model, and the data it was trained on. Many downloadable models publish only the first.

Because of this, whether some of these models count as open source is debated. Their licenses or missing training data may not meet common open-source definitions. When someone calls a model open, ask which of the three they mean and what the license allows.

[/ita_why]

Questions to answer before you choose

The right choice depends on the task and on your workplace's rules. Say an office admin at a dental clinic wants help summarizing internal notes. Before any model is chosen, someone has to decide whether those notes may leave the clinic's systems at all.

[ita_checklist id="open-or-closed-model"] [ita_item]We know what data will go into the model, and whether our rules allow it to leave our systems.[/ita_item] [ita_item]Someone is named to install, update and secure an open model, or we've chosen a hosted service instead.[/ita_item] [ita_item]We've read the license or service terms for our kind of use, including commercial use.[/ita_item] [ita_item]We've tested the model on a handful of our real tasks, with names removed.[/ita_item] [ita_item]We know what happens if the model version changes or is retired.[/ita_item] [/ita_checklist]

This article isn't legal advice. If the use is commercial or the license is unclear, ask a qualified person, such as your organization's legal team.
